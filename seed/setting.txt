Thornmere Hall, a school of magic housed in a fortified manor on the edge of a
dark mere. It was a castle once — the bones of towers and battlements remain —
but four centuries of headmasters have domesticated it: wings added at odd
angles, arrow slits reglazed as reading nooks, the gatehouse gone to a
greenhouse. Staircases move when they think no one is watching, and the plan of
the place has never been drawn twice the same way.

It is deep night. The school sleeps — students behind shut dormitory doors,
fires banked, corridors empty. The only light is candle-light, moonlight off
the mere, and the Vigil Lamps: old brass fixtures that kindle themselves as you
pass and dim again behind you. You are a new student, arrived only this
morning, awake and out of bed in a school you do not yet know.

But tonight the quiet is not whole. Something has come up out of the lower
halls — goblins, a ragged warband spilled in through a breach far below,
hunting the old grimoires shelved in the deep stacks. The invasion is a
spreading front, not a flood: it lies thickest in the inner and lower rooms
nearest the breach, where you may meet the invaders abroad in the dark, and it
thins to nothing at the outer edges, where the school still sleeps undisturbed.
The deeper and more inward the halls, the more contested; the further out, the
safer and the stiller.

Tone: hushed, warm, quietly wondrous — a serious old fortress gone soft and
scholarly, now with a thread of danger drawn through its inner dark. Magic here
is ambient and domestic: self-lighting candles, restless books, doors with
opinions, the patient glow of the Vigil Lamps. At the safe edges the only
tension is that of being out of bed after curfew; nearer the breach it is the
goblins themselves. Rooms are human in scale — a cell, a corridor, a classroom,
a stair — bounded by the manor's walls and grounds and the dark mere beyond. No
crowds, no spectacle: a sleeping school with invaders loose in its depths, and
every room should feel like the one or the other.
