The ruin of Elwyn Priory, a small monastic house abandoned generations ago and
now half-swallowed by the wood that grew up around it. Grey stone halls give way
to walled gardens gone feral; ivy and bramble reclaim what the monks once kept.
The air is cool and green-lit, the quiet that of a place emptied slowly rather
than sacked. Water still runs in old channels, and the plan of the place — hall,
cloister, garden, cell — remains legible under the moss to anyone who walks it.

Tone: hushed, elegiac, unhurried. The wonder here is of decay and persistence,
not of monsters or treasure. Nothing threatens; much is simply lost. Rooms are
human in scale — a hall, a garden, a passage, a chamber — connected by doorways,
stairs, and worn paths. Keep to that register: no vast caverns, no machinery, no
magic beyond the ordinary strangeness of an old holy place left to the seasons.
